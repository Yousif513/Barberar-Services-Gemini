# 13 — Business Strategy

> **Research date:** 2026-10-03. A recommended strategy for owner approval. Nothing has been implemented, priced, signed or launched. Labels: **KNOWN** · **BENCHMARK** · **ASSUMPTION** · **HYPOTHESIS** · **RECOMMENDATION**. Saudi legal points carry **REQUIRES LEGAL CONFIRMATION**.

---

## 1. Business model

PRIMORA should be a **hybrid: free-to-start booking software for Saudi salons and barbershops, plus a marketplace that sends them new clients, monetized mainly when the marketplace delivers.**

| Layer | Role | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Operations software (calendar, staff, policies, WhatsApp reminders, invoices) | Makes PRIMORA the provider's daily system of record | SaaS for teams from month 6 |
| Marketplace (discovery, reviews, booking) | Creates new demand | One-time new-client fee |
| Payments (deposits, prepayment, no-show fees via a licensed PSP) | Protects providers' revenue | Transparent processing; margin only if legally confirmed |
| Communications (WhatsApp-first) | Reduces no-shows; drives repeat bookings | Included utility; marketing at cost-plus |

**Why hybrid and not pure marketplace or pure SaaS:** a pure marketplace without software has no reason for providers to keep their calendars on it (leakage, stale availability). Pure SaaS puts PRIMORA head-on against Glamera's full ERP and licensed payments. The hybrid uses software to win supply and the marketplace to earn.

## 2. Marketplace model and customer ownership

| Question | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Who owns a client the provider brought? | **The provider.** 0 % commission; PRIMORA never markets competing providers to that client inside the provider's own booking page |
| Who owns a client PRIMORA's marketplace found? | **Shared:** the provider gets the client relationship after the first visit; PRIMORA keeps the marketplace account and may show that customer other providers in discovery |
| How is it determined? | Source tag on every booking + first-visit detection per provider–customer pair; disputes resolved on evidence (StyleSeat precedent) |
| What stops leakage? | Nothing to avoid: after the first visit, bookings are free. Contact details masked in chat before the first booking |
| Ranking | Transparent factors (distance, availability, rating with minimum count, response reliability); sponsored placement labelled |
| Quality floor | CR-verified, real photos and prices, ≥ 60 % of hours bookable, provider cancellation < 3 % |

## 3. Pricing strategy (summary of report 14)

- Launch: **Founding Partner** — free software 6 months; 0 % on own clients; first-visit fee 20 % (min SAR 10, max SAR 40); processing at cost.
- Growth: Solo SAR 0; Team SAR 149 / branch / month (≤ 10 professionals); Team Plus SAR 249 (≤ 25).
- Mature: sponsored placement, AI receptionist add-on, enterprise contracts.
- **All HYPOTHESES** to validate by interviews and cohort tests; the live pricing page is unchanged.

## 5. Provider acquisition (summary of report 16)

First 10 founder-led → first 50 by referral and district clusters → first 100 with two field sellers (male for barbershops, female for women's salons) → 500 through self-serve, WhatsApp ads to owners and the **ZATCA Wave 25** campaign → 1,000 via partner channels and second city.

## 6. Customer acquisition (summary of report 16)

Provider-driven first (links/QR on every mirror and WhatsApp status) → click-to-WhatsApp ads per district → Arabic local SEO → referral credits → seasonal campaigns (Ramadan nights, pre-Eid) → licensed influencers. No broad consumer advertising before district density.

## 10. Competitive positioning

| Competitor | Their strength (BENCHMARK) | Their weakness | PRIMORA position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fresha (KSA)** | Global product depth; free marketplace for returning clients | **50 %** new-client fee in KSA (2.5× its US rate); English-first; WhatsApp at SAR 0.15–0.30 per message | Same fairness on regulars, far cheaper new clients, Arabic and WhatsApp native |
| **Glamera** | Full ERP (POS, inventory, accounting), licensed payments, ZATCA Phase 1 & 2, ~4,500 providers, SAR 4B+ processed, acquired Bookr | Business-software DNA; marketplace secondary; pricing for marketplace undisclosed | Demand + simple operations; not an ERP |
| **SPOT (Slotex)** | Arabic WhatsApp reminders, assisted setup, salon pilot results | No public pricing; no consumer marketplace evident | SPOT-level operations + marketplace + public prices |
| **Samha / Laha / Jamal / Glamiva** | Women-first consumer apps, curated salons | Small scale; women-only; limited business software | Men's grooming first; family booking; verified businesses |
| **Booksy / StyleSeat / SQUIRE** | Mature US playbooks | KSA presence UNABLE TO VERIFY | Borrow the playbook (first-visit fees, booking links, portfolios), localize it |

**One-line position:** *The fair, Arabic, WhatsApp-native way for Saudi salons and barbershops to run their book and get new clients.*

## 11. Competitive moat

| Moat | How it compounds | What builds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Local density** | More providers per district → better choice → more customers → more providers | District-by-district launch |
| **Verified reputation data** | Reviews tied to completed bookings, per professional, cannot be imported elsewhere | Verified reviews ✓ + pro ratings |
| **Provider workflow lock-in (earned)** | Calendar, client notes, policies, invoices live in PRIMORA | Operations software, client import, ZATCA |
| **Fair-fee trust** | Providers recommend PRIMORA to each other | 0 % on own clients; transparent fees |
| **Cultural fit** | Prayer-aware scheduling, family booking, gender journeys, Ramadan/Eid tooling — hard for global players to prioritize | Already partly built |
| **WhatsApp operations data** | Confirm/reschedule behaviour, no-show prediction | WhatsApp-first flows → later AI |
| **Professional network** | Barbers and stylists with followers bring clients wherever they work | Portable pro profiles (later) |

Not a moat: features (copyable), price alone (Glamera and Fresha can cut), and the current codebase's breadth (much of it is UI only).

## 12. KPI framework (summary of report 17)

North star: **completed bookings per month**, with **marketplace-new share** beside it. Supporting: live providers, provider 90-day retention, no-show rate on reminded bookings, slot → paid conversion, 60-day customer repeat, CAC payback, unreconciled transactions (must be 0), critical security findings (must be 0).
